What will we sell, which deals need attention, and why?

Kairo runs machine-learning models over a CRM every day: it scores leads, estimates each deal's real chance of winning, forecasts revenue and checks that forecast against history, groups customers, recommends the next best action and writes short AI briefings for the most important deals.

Simulated data. The CRM is a realistic simulation of a B2B software company. Every company, person and deal is made up.

Overview

Revenue forecast, tested against history

Every two weeks in the past, the forecast was rebuilt using only the data available on that day, then compared with the revenue that actually closed. The usual CRM approach — multiplying each deal by the rep's own probability — is shown for comparison.

Open pipeline

Reps are optimistic. Weighting each open deal by the model's win probability instead of the rep's estimate gives a very different picture, stage by stage.

Deals that need attention

The open deals where attention is worth the most, ranked by dollars at stake × urgency. Open a deal to see the AI briefing, the risks and the recommended next step.

Hottest leads

Each open lead gets a score from 0 to 100: its estimated chance of becoming a deal, with the reasons behind it.

Customer segments

Customers grouped by K-means clustering on what they've bought, how recently, how engaged they are and whether they have an open deal.

Next best actions

One recommended action for every open lead, open deal and customer. Revenue actions are ranked by value at stake; hygiene actions keep the CRM (and the forecast) honest.

How it works

The daily pipeline

  1. Simulate — the CRM advances by one day: new leads, calls, emails, meetings, deals moving (and slipping).
  2. Load & clean — into DuckDB; duplicate contacts merged, gaps labelled.
  3. Snapshot — every open deal and lead as it looked on each past day, so models only ever learn from what was known at the time.
  4. Model — lead scoring, win probability, revenue forecast (scikit-learn), customer segments, next best actions.
  5. Brief — Google Gemini turns each top deal's facts into a short briefing.
  6. Publish — results are exported to one JSON file behind this static page.

Honest limitations

  • The data is simulated. Real CRMs are messier in ways a simulator can't fully capture.
  • In the simulation, buyers don't react to reps' actions, so next-best actions rank where attention is worth most; they don't measure how much an action helps.
  • The forecast learned from about 18 months of history. Its earliest backtests had little data to learn from, and its accuracy should improve as history grows.
  • AI briefings only restate facts the pipeline computed; the model is told never to invent anything.
